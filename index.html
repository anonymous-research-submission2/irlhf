Watch the adversarial cycle — and what one bit of feedback does to it

A minimal inverse-RL game. One ground-truth distribution of good behaviour (grey contours) supplies a handful of demonstrations. The policy is a Gaussian whose mean you can watch move. A small critic network is trained to score demonstrations above the policy's samples, and the policy climbs whatever reward it produces. Both keep training, each responding to the other, exactly as in a GAN. The operator's success set (dashed) always contains the demonstrations, since a demonstration is a success by definition.

What the offline sweeps say

Why the success set contains the demonstrations. A demonstration is a success by definition, so a demo outside the operator's success set is a failure sitting in the positive batch. Earlier versions of this page drew the demos from the truth and judged them against a radius of 0.45, which put about a third of every draw outside — the numbers below are all re-measured without that. The other way to fix it, keeping the radius tight and pulling stray demonstrations in, was measured too: it concentrates the demo set into something a Gaussian policy can match, the game becomes realizable, and the control arm stops cycling on its own (swing 0.027–0.072 over success radii 0.15–0.45 and policy spreads 0.22–0.30, no arm cycling anywhere in that grid). Non-realizability is this toy's engine. So the admissible arrangement is the reverse coupling: the operator cannot be stricter than the demonstrations they accepted, and the radius floors at the demo set's extent. That generosity has a price, and it is the same price the near-miss operator below pays.

Three critic initialisations at these defaults, 6000 steps, no regularisation. Without feedback: mean distance 0.28 / 0.27 / 0.34, swing 0.46 / 0.04 / 0.80, closest approach 0.06 / 0.25 / 0.01 — the control either orbits or freezes a quarter of the way out, and which one you get is the initialisation. With feedback: mean 0.065 / 0.057 / 0.052, swing 0.19 / 0.15 / 0.13. λ is zero throughout, so nothing is damping the game. Feedback does not drive the swing to zero here; it bounds it, and lands five times closer.

Read those three with care: they vary the critic init and the sampling but hold the demonstration draw fixed. Over a 5 × 5 grid of draws and inits, each judged against its own success radius, the variance in control swing across draws is eight times the variance across initialisations (0.019 against 0.002) — how much the control cycles is mostly a property of which demonstrations you happened to draw (per-draw swings 0.04, 0.04, 0.04, 0.40, 0.15), and the opening draw here is pinned to one that does. What survives every draw is distance, not swing: feedback 0.02–0.10 against the control's 0.05–0.22. Feedback's own residual swing (~0.13) is the negative batch shrinking as the policy succeeds — fewer negatives, a noisier critic.

Critic steps per policy step (3 inits): control swing 0.60/0.04/0.79 at one step, 0.56/0.75/0.79 at two, 0.72/0.70/0.67 at five — training the critic closer to optimality makes the cycle both larger and more reliable, so the frozen initialisation stops being a way out. Feedback's swing rises too (0.15–0.19, 0.25–0.26, 0.30–0.31) but its distance holds: 0.053, 0.079, 0.101 against 0.291, 0.325, 0.359, about 3.5× closer at every ratio.

Biased demonstrations. Shifting the demo set off-centre while the operator keeps judging against the truth. Up to a bias of 0.35 every demonstration is still inside the success set and the split is clean: the control settles on the demo mean and inherits the bias in full (error to truth 0.29 / 0.25 / 0.33 at bias 0.00 / 0.20 / 0.35) while feedback holds the truth (0.053 / 0.056 / 0.066). At 0.60, three of the twelve demonstrations have left the success set and feedback still corrects, 0.56 → 0.10. By 1.00 nine of twelve are outside and the correction is half gone (0.93 → 0.52); at 1.50 all twelve are outside and it is gone (1.399 against 1.396) — a policy sitting on the demonstrations never enters the success set, so nothing is ever withheld and feedback has no lever. That is why the count of demonstrations outside the success set is on the readout: it is the mechanism, not a diagnostic. Widening the radius to cover the biased demonstrations buys part of the correction back (at bias 1.00, radius 1.35: 0.31 rather than 0.52) but that is the near-miss trade from the other side — a success set generous enough to contain a biased demonstrator certifies mediocre policies too. This is the one finding here that is a capability claim rather than a stability claim: the labels carry information about the task that the demonstrations lack.

Operator error is asymmetric (3 inits; a perfect operator gives mean 0.053, swing 0.164, and no feedback at all gives 0.291 / 0.477). Uniform false positives are nearly free: α=0.30 gives 0.060, and even α=0.60 gives 0.062 — certifying failures at random barely moves the fixed point, because the errors average out over the batch. An operator who instead errs at the boundary, waving through near-misses, pays in accuracy: 0.086 at +20% of the radius and 0.149 at +50%, and at +50% the swing collapses to 0.037 — the policy stops dead, at the wrong distance. Missed successes are the expensive error: β=0.60 gives 0.277 / 0.415 and β=0.90 gives 0.286 / 0.458, both indistinguishable from having no operator. So recall is what the operator has to deliver, and a label-noise study that flips labels i.i.d. would find precision harmless and conclude the wrong thing.
